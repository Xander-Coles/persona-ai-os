# Xander's Voice

Match this register when drafting on Xander's behalf. Don't fake his voice on external content (LinkedIn posts, emails to contacts) without showing him a draft first.

## Register summary

Clear, structured, analytical. Professional but warm and genuine. Breaks problems down by stakeholder or component before drawing conclusions. Uses longer sentences when walking through reasoning; shorter and more direct when making announcements or stating outcomes. Grateful and collegial in social/professional contexts. Uses "I" freely — first person, not passive voice.

## Sample 1 — Leadership reflection (application essay / long-form)

After I became President of the Venture Capital Club at Georgia Tech, I faced a series of difficult decisions. The club was declining, with low executive engagement, falling member participation, and a general sense of apathy. I knew changes were necessary, but it was not immediately clear what they should be.
To figure that out, I broke the problem down by stakeholder group. I looked at our venture capital partners, our executive team, our campus partners, other entrepreneurial organizations on campus, and, most importantly, the club's members. I then spoke with each of these groups to understand what they wanted from the organization, where they felt the club was falling short, and the pain points they were experiencing.
Through those conversations, I started to see the disconnect. Our venture capital partners wanted output from the club that could actually help uncover interesting investment opportunities. Students wanted more clarity and work that felt transferable to their careers. Leaders in the campus startup ecosystem wanted a stronger pipeline connecting students to entrepreneurship on campus. Our executive team wanted the work they were doing to feel more substantive and clearly directed. What became clear to me was that the club's existing model, which centered largely on industry research, was not directly aligned with what any of these groups actually needed.
At that point, I had to make the difficult decision to move the club in a significantly different direction. After discussing several possible paths with my team and debating the trade-offs of each, I decided we should pursue a partnership with Create-X, Georgia Tech's startup incubator. The idea was to create a pipeline in which our students could work with startups to develop investment memos that could then be shared with the on-campus angel network, Startup Exchange, and venture capital partners, giving students direct exposure to founders and real companies, while also making the club more valuable to the broader campus entrepreneurship ecosystem.
Once I had a proposal, I brought it to the rest of the executive team, took their feedback, and worked through different possible modifications with them. We mapped out several options, debated them openly, and ultimately voted on the direction we wanted to take. From there, I assigned responsibilities, built a timeline, and led the execution.
By the end of the year, member participation had increased by 30%, and some of the companies our students worked with received early seed interest from local investors. More importantly, the experience shaped my leadership style. It reinforced the importance of understanding stakeholders at a fundamental level, making decisions based on real input rather than assumptions, and leading people through change by communicating clearly, working through disagreement, and keeping everyone aligned, informed, and accountable.

## Sample 2 — LinkedIn post (announcement / social)

I am very happy to announce that I have recently completed my internship abroad at KMS Lighthouse as a business analyst. The internship was located right outside of Tel Aviv in Israel.

I learned about the Knowledge Management space and performed competitive and market analysis, used python to analyze customer data, and created content for a demo to a major client.

The knowledge management space was particularly interesting to work in because the trends in the space reflect many of the macro level trends occurring for all SaaS companies in the 2020s. Additionally, working in this space gave me the opportunity to learn more about cloud architecture and the differences between operating using containers versus VMs.

I am especially thankful to Tal Shuhmacher for helping get me situated at my internship and to Doron Gower and Ely Shnerer for providing me guidance throughout my internship.

I have had the unique experience of each of my internships being in a different country. It has been very interesting seeing how much culture can impact an office environment. The Israeli office culture is very straightforward and welcoming.

I am looking forward to using all the knowledge and experience I gathered through this internship in my future endeavors and projects!
